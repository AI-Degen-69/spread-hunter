Why the rehearsal bought nothing — and what you see now

For three days the rehearsal kept placing orders and never bought a single share. We found the reason: every order was parked behind a line of shares already waiting at the very same price. The report now shows you that line, out loud, and the market filter stops throwing away the good markets.

The line in front of your order

An order only fills after everyone already waiting at its price has been served. The rehearsal refuses to guess: it credits a buy only when real trades actually printed at that exact price and cleared the whole line first. So if the line is enormous, "no buy" is the honest answer — not a broken system, and not bad luck either.

Here is the worst case from the recorded rehearsal: a size-5 order with 38,706 shares waiting ahead of it. The market must trade about 7,741 times your order size before your first share is reached. Press Step and watch the line eat those trades.

The line, draining as real trades print
Step: 0 / 40 — 0 of 38,706 shares served
Watch the dark bar shrink from the left: each bite is trade volume arriving at that price. Your orange block at the far right only lights up when the line is completely gone.

Nothing here is a fault. The rehearsal recorded 4 orders, 0 buys — and its line ran up to 7,741× your size. Zero buys was the correct answer to that question. The old report simply never showed the line, so it looked like a mystery.

What the report shows now

Every rehearsal report ends with a Queue depth section. It reports how many quotes could be measured, the middle and worst size of the line, and — when the rehearsal only got through a single decision pass — a plain sentence saying that no buys are expected. That last line is the one that would have answered the three-day silence on day one.

The filter that let too much go

Markets arrive grouped under a name — and one safety rule refused any market with a group name, meant only to block sub-markets. So "Brazil", "Donald Trump" and "December 31, 2027" — ordinary main events — were turned away before any liquidity check ever ran. On one ranking that produced 95 refusals and left 5 usable markets out of 188.

The rule now refuses only labels that actually name a fragment, and lets plain names through to the usual volume, depth, spread and timing checks.

Still refused — real fragments

  • Spread -3.5
  • Handicap +4
  • Game 1
  • Map 3 — Round 2
  • Over/Under 45.5
  • 0.62 – 0.65 price band

Now allowed — main-line names

  • Brazil
  • Donald Trump
  • December 31, 2027
  • Lakers
  • Republican
  • Alabama vs Mississippi State

27 → 45 markets now pass on the same frozen 71-market snapshot — 67% more to choose from. All 95 previously-blocked labels were re-checked one by one: only date labels got through, and every true fragment is still refused.

Try it yourself

Run a rehearsal from the menu and open the report it writes. Scroll to the bottom — you will now find the Queue depth section with your run's own middle and worst line, plus the single-pass note when the run was short. While the rehearsal runs, the market list shows the newly-admitted group names instead of dropping them.
