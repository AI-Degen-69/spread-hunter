The job I'd spend the budget on — and why the obvious one is a trap

You asked: with budget for a top-tier model, what is the most capable job to give on Spread Hunter? Here is the answer, the evidence behind it, and the reasoning that disqualified the alternatives — including the fix that looks correct and measurably makes the engine worse.

243 closes measured 1 shadow window · 167 markets · 331 pairs no signer · nothing spent net −$9.99 counterfactual n = 18
The job

Fill quality → entry gate: prove the edge survives adverse selection

Build the per-fill attribution that shows which fills become dead legs and why, then gate entry on that evidence — and prove the gate in a shadow A/B judged on inclusive PnL, never merge-only.

One sentence of why: the merge business is profitable (+$56.95), the stop-loss is doing its job (it saved $7.49), and 100% of the loss is legs that were bought and then died — so the only lever with money in it is the decision to own the leg at all.

The 49 losing closes break down as 32 hard stops (−$93.56), 13 losing rescue exits (−$5.96) and 4 losing settlements (−$7.35) — which is why the settlement path nets positive (+$25.44) while still containing losers.

The asymmetry that sets the whole agenda

A merge earns $0.33. A hard stop costs $2.92. One dead leg erases 8.9 merges — so this is not a book where you can win by improving the good half. You win by shrinking the bad half, or not at all.

2The trap: the obvious fix loses money

The first instinct on seeing −$93.56 from a stop-loss is to soften it, widen the floor, or wait the leg out. That is the fix a cheap model reaches for, and it is wrong. The store can answer it directly: for every hard stop whose market later resolved, we know both branches — what the stop realized, and what the leg would have been worth at settlement.

Verdict — do not touch the stop

Stopping saved $7.49 on the cases we can measure. Softening it, delaying it, or holding to resolution all convert a bounded loss into a larger one. The stop is not the bug — it is the only thing currently limiting the bug.

This is the highest-value thing the analysis produced: it kills the most attractive wrong answer before anyone spends a week implementing it.

3So the loss is upstream — anatomy of one dead leg

If the exit is not the problem, the problem must be the entry: something got bought that should not have been. Step through the five stages of a leg that dies. Every figure is measured from the 32 hard stops in the store.

Quote — both legs rest below mid

A binary market, mid 0.84 / 0.16. The bot posts offset prices.

UP is quoted at 0.82, DOWN at 0.14. If both fill, the pair costs 0.96 and the merge returns $1.00 — four cents of edge per share, which is the entire thesis. Quote size is 120 shares per leg, up to three markets at a time.

Nothing is wrong here yet. This stage is where all 173 winning merges come from.

Fill — only the leg somebody wanted to sell you

Adverse selection in one sentence: you get filled on the leg the market is leaving.

In the 32 hard stops, the leg that filled cost $0.486 per share on average across 7.84 shares. The population that goes on to die clusters in the expensive half of the book, not the cheap tail — but the tail dies too.

This is the stage the job targets. It is also the stage nothing currently measures: the fill ledger records that a fill happened, not what the book looked like when it happened.

One-sided — the hedge leg prices itself away

PATIENT_WAIT → ESCALATED_HEDGE → HARD_STOP.

The lifecycle waits for the opposite leg, then escalates the hedge. By then the book has moved: the cheap side is no longer cheap, and paying up for it would break the pair-cost-under-$1 rule that defines the strategy. So the leg is held naked until the floor fires.

The lifecycle machinery is working as designed. It is being fed a bad input.

Collapse — the leg marks down roughly 76%

Entry $0.486 → exit $0.112, and the ratio is strikingly constant.

Break the 32 stops out by entry price and the collapse is the same percentage in every bucket (ladder below). Cheap legs lose 76%. Expensive legs lose 76%. Only the dollar size of the position differs; the damage rate does not.

Stop — a mechanical haircut, not a decision

The emergency floor sits 75% below the entry average, so it books ≈ −77% every time.

That is why tuning the stop is futile: it has no way to tell a leg that will recover from one that will not, because it only looks at distance from entry. Whatever you set, it realizes that distance uniformly. You are choosing which fixed percentage to pay, not whether the leg was worth owning.

What that chart proves

The stop-loss has no information content. It fires at a fixed distance below entry, so it books the same percentage whether the leg was a $0.83 mistake or a $0.24 mistake. No parameter you change alters the outcome — only which constant you pay.

Therefore the only lever with real money attached is whether the leg is bought at all, and the only way to know that is to attribute outcomes back to fill conditions.

Two ways to read the same gap. In merges: 32 dead legs need 284 merges to pay for themselves; the window produced 173 — so only about 19 dead legs were affordable. In rates: the engine closed pairs 205 times (173 merges + 32 stops), and the stop-loss share needs to sit under 10.1% to break even. It sits at 15.6%.

The criterion that makes the job testable

A gate is only worth shipping if the legs it rejects are more than 8.9× more likely to die than to merge. That is a single number, computable from the store, and it converts "should we add a filter?" from taste into arithmetic. Anything weaker than 8.9× removes more profit than it saves, no matter how bad the rejected legs look.

This is also why the job must be a measurement job first. No one can guess which fill conditions clear 8.9×; it has to be calibrated, then checked, then shadow-tested.

5Two smaller levers the same work exposes

5a · 38% of merges earn almost nothing

Sort merges by what the pair cost. The cheap half does the work; the expensive tail barely pays for the risk it carries.

Why this is evidence, not a plan

It is tempting to bolt on a pair_cost < 0.98 filter today. Don't — not yet. That number is an artifact of this window and it is the same measurement the job produces properly. Ship a threshold before you have the per-fill attribution and you will be tuning the wrong variable with 173 observations. The job produces the calibration; the gate falls out of it.

5b · Dust that cannot be sold keeps hammering the exit

5,464 exit refusals across 10 pairs. The worst single pair logged 2,041 refusals in 68 minutes — one attempt every two seconds — because the position was 0.1087 shares and the venue will not take an order below 1.0 share. The engine already recognises this as a dust position; it just keeps retrying anyway.

This is a symptom, not a cause: it moves no PnL directly. But it buries real errors in noise, burns CPU, and would mask the gate's own telemetry during the A/B. It belongs in the same session as the main job, as the one cheap fix.

6The job, written as a spec

Fill quality → entry gate: prove the strategy's edge survives adverse selection. Measurement first, gate second, proof third. Read-only until the gate is justified.

Per-fill attribution report — core_brain/fill_quality_report.py, read-only

Join every fill to (a) the book state at fill time, (b) the lifecycle it became, and (c) the terminal outcome of that pair. One row per fill, one CLI entry point, no writes, no venue calls. This is the artifact that does not exist today — the fill ledger records that a fill happened, never what it looked like when it happened.

Blocks everything else. Without it, every gate proposal is guessing.

Calibration: P(leg worthless | conditions) with honest intervals

Bucket fills by the features that plausibly carry the adverse-selection signal — distance from mid, spread at fill, depth on the opposite side, time to resolution, gap between the two legs' fill times — and report death rate, merge rate, and lift versus base rate with confidence intervals.

A bucket supported by 5 observations is not a finding. The report must say so itself.

Proposed gate + break-even table, defaults off

For each candidate threshold, the table shows: legs rejected, merges lost, dead legs avoided, net PnL delta. A threshold ships only where the ratio clears 8.9:1. The gate goes in off-by-default so it cannot change live behaviour by accident.

Turns "add a filter" into a decision the operator reads off one table.

Shadow A/B on inclusive PnL — never merge-only

Same window, gate on versus off, judged on total realized PnL including stops and settlements. Merge-only scoring flatters every filter that reduces activity, because it hides the dead legs the filter did not prevent.

This is the exact metric that would have made the stop-tuning mistake obvious.

Dust fix + absence test

Stop retrying exits on positions below the venue minimum (0.1087 shares vs 1.0). Log once, then wait. Add a test asserting no repeated refusal loop for a dust position — the kind that fails today, which is the only kind worth writing here.

Cheap, and it keeps the A/B telemetry readable.

✗ This is not done if…

  • the report exists but nothing is gated;
  • a gate ships without a break-even table;
  • the A/B is scored on merges alone;
  • the stop-loss, sizing, or tournament parameters were touched;
  • "no gate exists" was concluded but never written down as a result.

✓ This is a win if…

  • we can name which fills die and by how much, with intervals;
  • a threshold clears 8.9:1 in shadow and survives the A/B;
  • or the same rigour shows no observable condition predicts the deaths — which redirects the search to sizing and market selection instead.

7Why this needs the strongest model, not the cheapest

This is the part of the answer that is about the budget. The job is not large in lines of code. It is large in judgement, in five specific ways:

TemptationWhy it costs real money here
Fix the thing that looks broken The stop-loss is the biggest negative line item and it is correct. Softening it loses $7.49 on the 18 measurable cases. A model that optimises the visible number actively damages the engine.
Trust the aggregate metric Markouts across 421 fills run 0.4748 → 0.4804 — mildly favourable. At the aggregate level these fills look fine. The bad population hides inside the average, so a naive dashboard prints "no problem" while 32 legs bleed $93.56.
Overfit small buckets 32 stops, 18 resolved counterfactuals, one pipeline bucket with 5 closes. A confident conclusion is one bad bucket away. The model must be willing to report "not enough data" as a result.
Measure the convenient way Merge-only PnL makes any activity-reducing gate look like a win. Inclusive PnL is the only honest scoreboard, and choosing it requires knowing why.
Refactor while you are in there trader_loop.py is 158 KB and sits upstream of both failure modes this repo exists to prevent: a pair assembled over $1.00, and an unhedged single leg. Scope discipline is the safety property, not tidiness.
The budget argument, short

A cheap model hands you a fix for the loudest number, and here the loudest number is a symptom that is already being handled correctly. The expensive part of this job is refusing four plausible answers and proving the fifth.

9What could change my mind

Weak points in this evidence

  • One window. 243 closes, 167 markets, a single shadow session. A different regime could reorder every bucket.
  • n = 18. The counterfactual rests on hard stops whose market later resolved. 11 of 18 would flip the sign; since only 1 of 18 recovered, the direction is safe and the magnitude is not.
  • Shadow fills are models, not the venue. Real adverse selection is usually worse than a fill model assumes — which strengthens the case for measuring at the fill.
  • The refusals may be shadow-specific. 5,464 is an upper bound.

Falsifiers — how the job can fail honestly

  • No bucket clears 8.9× with usable intervals → the gate does not exist, and the next job is market selection, not entry filtering.
  • All buckets look identical → deaths are driven by post-fill events, not fill conditions, and the lifecycle hedge path becomes the target.
  • A gate clears 8.9× in calibration then dies in the A/B → the calibration overfit this window, and the finding is "recalibrate on fresh shadow data".
