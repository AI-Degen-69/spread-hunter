SPREAD HUNTER · ISSUE #942 · DECISION RECAP

The conclusion: quarter Kelly — and it must be earned, not granted

Why the bot now sizes orders with a quarter of the Kelly fraction instead of half, what the research actually says, and the exact conditions under which it gets bigger.

⚖️
Verdict. The bot ships with bot_quote_kelly_pct = 0.25 (quarter Kelly) as the opt-in sizing dial; half Kelly is a setting you earn at ≥ 30 live closes with the edge holding. Below that: sizing is byte-identical to today’s dollars mode and the order says so. No edge ⇒ no orders. The existing caps stay binding.

1 — The physics in one line

Your bankroll doesn’t add from bet to bet — it multiplies, and multiplication is brutally asymmetric: a win and a loss of equal size do not cancel, they compound into a hole.

The 60% coin, 100 flips, $1,000

Bet it all every flip: the average outcome reads ≈ $83 billion — propped up by the single absurd universe where you never lose (odds ≈ 1 in 15 billion trillion). In every universe you’d actually live in, the first tails wipes you out.

Arithmetic mean ≠ typical outcome. Kelly optimizes the log (typical, compounding) outcome, not the average.

Win one, lose one — the "breakeven" that isn’t

Bet half your stack, win, then lose: 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75 — a quarter of everything is gone.

Same pair at 20% of stack: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 — close to even, and your edge can carry you forward.

Losses punch harder than equal wins. A single zero kills everything after it.

The trap: bet too much and this winning game grinds you to nothing; bet too little and your edge does nothing for you. The whole question is where between timid and reckless the exact number sits — that number is the Kelly fraction.

2 — The growth curve: peak at f*, cliff past 2f*

Expected log-growth per bet for a 60/40 even-money coin, as a function of bet size. Illustrative shape — the live bot’s f* comes from its own measured closes, and only the direction of the slope matters here.

Curve: g(f) = 0.6·ln(1+f) + 0.4·ln(1−f), computed for this chart — not hand-drawn. Left of the peak the slope is gentle; right of it the cliff drops fast. Half Kelly buys ~¾ of peak growth for half the exposure; past double Kelly the same winning coin is a losing game.

3 — The three pillars of the evidence

① Thorp — the asymmetry

Over-betting is always worse than under-betting. Half Kelly protects against negative growth at the cost of ≤ 25% lost growth; past ~2f* geometric growth goes negative.

Kelly (1956); Thorp, The Kelly criterion in blackjack, sports betting, and the stock market; MacLean/Thorp/Ziemba (2010).

② Crane — you must be able to tell luck from edge

On a real 10% edge over 1,000 rounds, how likely is a 50% drawdown? That’s the event that makes you question the edge — and whether it’s informative decides everything.

Full Kelly
46% · LR 2.1
Half Kelly
11% · LR 7.4
Quarter Kelly
0.8% · LR 75

LR = likelihood ratio that the drawdown means “no edge”. Full Kelly: you’ll can’t act on it (2.1× — ambiguous). Quarter Kelly: LR = 75 — near-conclusive. Sizing smaller doesn’t pander to fear; it makes the evidence actionable. “In practice, we never know everything we need to know.”

③ Poluri — earn your Kelly

Optimal fraction of Kelly ≈ S / (S+1), where the evidence score S = N·k² (N = closed trades, k = standardized edge).

fraction ≈ S/(S+1),  S = N·k²
half Kelly justified at ≈ 0.7/k² trades

Poluri, How Many Trades Before You Trust Kelly? (SSRN 7549165, 2026). Half Kelly is earned by data, not chosen by taste — with a thin sample, even half is mathematically unjustified.

The consensus, not a clique: Wikipedia’s Kelly article notes practice favors fractional Kelly for drawdown reasons; Downey’s simulations find fractional Kelly “overdetermined” — no single justification you can dismiss; Crane shows the decision value (LR 75); Poluri gives the dose-response curve (S/(S+1)); the video your operator linked says the same thing in intuition form — “betting half of a guess is how you stay on the safe slope of being wrong.”

5 — The decision, exactly

  1. Quarter Kelly is the shipped default. bot_quote_kelly_pct = 0.25, hard cap 1.0 — a setting above 1 clamps to 1, it never levers.
  2. Below 30 bot-owned closes, sizing is byte-identical to today. bot_quote_kelly_min_closes = 30; the posted order carries ; kelly fallback: insufficient sample (n closes) so the audit trail shows which world you were in.
  3. Measured f* ≤ 0 ⇒ quote nothing. The no-edge case returns size 0 with the reason in the quote diagnostics — the fraction multiplier can never rescue a non-edge (Crane: fractional Kelly only delays ruin if there’s no edge).
  4. Half Kelly is earned, not defaulted. At n ≥ 30 live closes with the edge holding, the operator may promote the knob to 0.5. Re-check every +25 closes.
  5. Full Kelly (1.0) is shadow/rehearsal only — an operator rule, not a code gate (config can’t see live vs shadow). Live caps at ≤ 0.25.
  6. Kelly rides inside the existing caps, never past them — 6% naked / 25% order / 90% total / pair-cost cap / venue floor / (1−u)² taper all unchanged.
  7. Opt-in only. BOT_QUOTE_SIZE_MODE=kelly; the shipped default stays dollars until you flip it hands-on. Nothing changes live until you say so.

6 — What you’ll see, and the path to bigger size

Where it lives: core_brain/config.py (default 0.25), overridable via BOT_QUOTE_KELLY_PCT in .env (documented in .env.example). Sizing math: alloc = bankroll × min(f*, 1.0) × 0.25, floored at the venue minimum, tapered by the naked-budget rule, capped by the existing dynamic caps.

On orders: posted quotes in fallback mode carry the marker ; kelly fallback: insufficient sample (n closes); refused quotes say kelly: no edge (f*=…, n=… closes) or name the venue-floor shortfall.

Now — Quarter Kelly ships. Mode off by default; your risk gates and sizes are untouched until you opt in.
n ≥ 30 live closes — Kelly engages (no more dollars fallback). Check the measured f* on the dashboard; if it’s negative, the bot already refuses to bet.
Every +25 closes — re-derive the fraction from live data only. Decay ⇒ the sizing self-silences by design.
Promote to 0.5 (half Kelly) — only if the edge held across those closes and drawdown tracked the projection. Not before — not from paper, not from hope.
Falsifier — n ≥ 100 live closes, f* > 0, realized drawdown ≪ quarter-Kelly projection → the operator may argue for 0.5. Otherwise the default stays 0.25 indefinitely.
The cost of this decision: slower compounding while the edge is unproven — deliberately. The cost of the other decision is a ruined bankroll that never got the chance to learn whether it had an edge. Growth doesn’t go to whoever wins the most flips; it goes to whoever is still there to flip again.
