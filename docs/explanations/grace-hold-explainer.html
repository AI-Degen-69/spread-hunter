Why “grace” keeps your orders alive

The refusal-hold path in core_brain/trader_loop.py — what it is, why it fires, and how it differs from the rescue grace you removed.

Short answer: there are two different things called “grace” in this codebase. You removed grace #1 (rescue patience on filled legs — dead, no reader). The thing holding your orders is grace #2 (hold-on-refusal, issue #390) — a quote-management rule that keeps resting orders through up to 3 consecutive refused cycles before cancelling. It is live, deliberate, and unrelated to the stop-loss spec.

1. The two graces — dead vs live

GRACE #1 — DEAD

Rescue patience (single_buy_grace_sec)

Gave a filled single leg N seconds as maker before rescue. Retired in #619: the env var now logs “retired and ignored” (config.py:1494-1511) and no code reads the knob. This is the one you remember removing. It cannot hold anything.

Leftover mentions: reason="grace_expired" labels on old closes, dashboard chips, two open cleanup rows — forensic text, zero behavior.

GRACE #2 — LIVE

Hold-on-refusal (REFUSED_HOLD_GRACE_CYCLES = 3)

When the loop visits a market but decide refuses to quote (book flicker, not abandonment), resting orders are kept, not cancelled — for up to 3 consecutive refused cycles. Lives in trader_loop.py:128 and fires at :456-461.

Nothing to do with filled legs, rescue, or the stop-loss MD. It manages unfilled resting quotes.

4. Why it exists (the churn it prevents)

From the comment at trader_loop.py:85-88: on shadow-02, 23 of 25 orders were cancelled with a median lifetime of 37s — mostly book flicker, not real abandonment. Cancelling on every refused cycle means:

  • paying cancel/replace churn on every book wobble,
  • losing queue position each time, then re-joining at the back.

The 3-cycle hold says: “the book moved, not the market — sit still until it's clearly dead.” A market that never comes back still cancels on the 3rd consecutive refusal. The counter is per-market (refused_streaks[cid]), so one dead market can't spend another's patience.
